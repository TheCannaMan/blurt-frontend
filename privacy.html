Privacy & Terms

Last updated: August 2026
Blurt is a personal project built and run by one person, currently being tested by a small group of people the developer knows directly. This document is written in plain language to be genuinely honest about what the app does — it is not a substitute for formal legal review, and it will be revisited properly before Blurt is ever offered more broadly or commercially.

What Blurt does with your data

Your shopping list, recipes, and settings live on your own device by default (in your browser's local storage) and never touch our servers unless you choose to sign in with Google.

If you sign in with Google, your list, recipes, and settings sync to a private database (Firestore, part of Google Firebase) tied to your Google account, so they follow you across devices. Only you can access this data — it's locked to your account. We receive your Google account's name and email as part of that sign-in, and use it for nothing beyond identifying which synced data is yours.

Your location is handled deliberately conservatively. If you use "Use my location," your device's GPS coordinates are used only for that browsing session to find your nearest store, then discarded — never stored anywhere. If you type a ZIP code and check "remember this ZIP," that ZIP code (nothing more precise) is saved on your own device so you don't have to re-enter it — it is never sent to us for storage, only used to ask Kroger's API for nearby stores each time you shop.

What you search for — the items and recipes you type or say — is sent to our backend, which forwards it to Kroger's product API to get real prices, and to TheMealDB for recipe search. We don't log, store, or build any profile from your search history.

Shared lists — if you create or join a shared list, its items and name are visible to everyone you've shared it with (and no one else), stored the same way as your personal data, tied to your Google account since collaboration requires knowing who's involved.

Crash reporting. We use Sentry to catch and fix bugs — when something breaks, technical details (what happened, not who you are) are sent to Sentry so it can be fixed. This runs on both our backend and in the app itself.

Basic usage counters. We log simple, anonymous counts of which features get used (e.g. "an item was added," "shopping mode was started") to understand what's actually useful and what isn't. These are just an event name and a timestamp — never tied to your identity, never building a profile of you individually, and never sold or shared. This is different from third-party analytics or ad-tech tracking: it's our own minimal, first-party counting, and nobody but the developer can see it.

We don't run ads, sell data, or use any third-party advertising or behavioral tracking.

Feedback submitted through the in-app feedback button opens your own email app — we only receive what you choose to send us directly. Occasionally, after a shopping session, the app may show a quick optional "how did it go?" prompt — always dismissible, never required, and never shown more than once every few days.

Services Blurt relies on

  • Kroger — for real product and store data. Governed by Kroger's own privacy policy.
  • Google Firebase — for optional sign-in, cross-device sync, shared lists, and our own basic usage counters. Governed by Google's privacy policy.
  • TheMealDB — for recipe search.
  • Sentry — for crash/error reporting, on both the backend and in the app itself.
  • Render — hosts the app and its backend.

Deleting your data

Since most of your data lives on your own device, clearing your browser's site data for blurt.works deletes it completely. If you've signed in with Google and want your synced cloud data deleted too, use the feedback button to ask — it'll be removed by hand, since there's no automated account-deletion flow yet at this stage.

Not for children

Blurt isn't directed at children and we don't knowingly collect data from anyone under 13.
